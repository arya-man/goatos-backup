PHC VACCINATION IN GOAT OS
Operating Stories for Manager Approval

Document purpose
This document explains how vaccination scheduling and shed drives work in Goat OS,
written as real farm situations. It is meant for PHC and operations leadership to
read, challenge, and approve before we rely on the system in staging or
production.

What we are not doing
We are not picking a shed and vaccinating every animal inside it on one day
without checking what each animal actually needs. That was the old risk. Goat OS
works differently: each animal gets its own due doses first; only then does the
system group safe, eligible work into shed drives.


================================================================================
PART ONE — THE NORMAL LIFE OF A FARM-BORN KID
================================================================================

A kid is born in Shed 12 on your farm.

When the kid is registered in Goat OS, the system records that it was born here,
not bought from outside. It uses the date of birth to work out when each vaccine
is due. If we do not know whether the mother was vaccinated, we treat the mother
as vaccinated and use the later kid timings (for example PPR around sixteen weeks,
not four weeks). That is the policy we are asking you to confirm.

The first dose due is usually ET+TT, around four weeks of age. The system creates
a piece of work for that kid: "this animal owes ET+TT primary by this date." That
work shows on the goat's passport, on protocol adherence, and on the calendar
when the date is near. Nothing is given automatically; the system only plans and
tracks.

Three weeks after the first ET+TT is accepted, the booster is due. When PHC or
the operator records the first dose and verification accepts it, the system
schedules the booster itself. Nobody has to remember to add the next date by hand.

The kid then moves through the series in order: PPR, pox (goat pox for goats,
sheep pox for sheep), and later FMD and HS when those rows become due. Live
vaccines are not squeezed together if the gap rules would be broken. For example,
if PPR was given recently, goat pox may be pushed to a later week so the four-week
live-to-live gap is respected.

When FMD and HS are both due in the same window, the system tries to plan them
for the same shed visit. They are still two vaccine products, and we never put
more than two vaccines on one combo visit.

As the kid grows up and finishes the young-animal series, adult repeat doses take
over: ET+TT every six months, FMD every nine months, and so on, based on the
published rules in Config. Each repeat is triggered from the last accepted dose,
not from birth again.


================================================================================
PART TWO — WHEN A SHED DRIVE HAPPENS (AND WHO IS ON IT)
================================================================================

Several kids in Shed 12 are due for PPR in the same week. They are healthy, not
in warming, not in ICU, and not in late pregnancy. Their work is open and due.

Overnight or on schedule, the system runs a planning pass for vaccination drives.
It looks only at animals whose work is due or scheduled, not on hold. It groups
goats in the same shed that need the same vaccine in the same time window. Goats
and sheep are never mixed in one drive even if they share a shed.

If Shed 12 has enough animals, one PPR drive is created for that shed. A date is
picked inside the medical window. Vaccine stock is reserved using first-expiry
first-out. A vaccination SOP task is opened for the shed team.

On drive day, the operator sees the list of goats on that task, gives PPR to
each, records doses and proof. PHC verifies. Each accepted dose closes that
goat's PPR obligation and the system plans what comes next for that animal
individually.

If Shed 12 has only one goat due and another shed in the same park also has only
one goat due for the same vaccine, the system may combine them into one park-level
visit instead of sending a truck for a single animal. If no combination is
possible, a small drive still runs for that one goat so it is not forgotten.

Animals on hold never appear on a drive list until the hold is cleared and their
work is reopened.


================================================================================
PART THREE — A YOUNG ANIMAL BOUGHT FROM OUTSIDE
================================================================================

A ten-week-old kid arrives on a procurement load.

The system marks it as procured, not farm-born. Because it is under sixteen weeks,
it still follows the full kid schedule from date of birth, not the short adult
intake schedule. That matters: young purchases are not treated like mature adults.

For the first seven days after arrival, warming applies. No vaccination is
planned or executed, even if someone says the animal was vaccinated at the
source farm. Work shows as on hold for warming. After day seven, normal kid
scheduling continues.

If trusted records show ET+TT was already given at source with proper evidence,
the system does not create a duplicate ET+TT obligation. It moves on to the next
missing step in the series. If the source notes are not trusted, the intake
schedule applies and PHC may need to review catch-up.


================================================================================
PART FOUR — AN ADULT ANIMAL BOUGHT FROM OUTSIDE
================================================================================

A two-year-old breeding goat arrives.

It is past the kid cutoff, so it enters the adult procurement path. After the
seven-day warming hold, the first intake step is ET+TT and PPR together (two
products, one visit). Four weeks later, goat pox and ET+TT booster are due
together where the rules allow. FMD and HS follow on their repeat cycles.

This animal does not go back through the week-by-week kid table. That is
intentional: adults are on the shorter arrival schedule.


================================================================================
PART FIVE — WHEN HEALTH STOPS VACCINATION FOR A WHILE
================================================================================

A kid in Shed 12 is due for a booster next week. Before drive day, the animal
becomes sick.

The health status is updated in Goat OS. The system rechecks that goat. Open
vaccination work is put on hold with a clear reason: sick. The goat drops off the
drive list. Calendar and adherence show deferred work, not a silent skip.

A week later the kid recovers. Health is updated again. The system reopens the
held work. If another drive in that shed is planned within about seven days, the
goat can be aligned to that visit. If not, work becomes due immediately so a
small drive can be planned and the animal is not left waiting indefinitely.

The same pattern applies for ICU, quarantine, under treatment, and for animals
moved into ICU or quarantine sheds: hold while the risk applies, reopen when the
farm confirms recovery or move back to normal care.


================================================================================
PART SIX — PREGNANCY AND AFTER KIDDING
================================================================================

A female on the farm becomes pregnant while she still has open vaccination work.

In months one to three, vaccination can continue if she is otherwise healthy and
eligible. In months four and five, doses that would fall in that window are held
for late pregnancy. The hold is visible on adherence.

After she kids, there is a short catch-up window (two weeks in the current rules)
when missed doses from the pregnancy hold can be brought forward. After that
window, scheduling returns to normal for her stage.

If breeding date is missing, pregnancy month may show as needing review until
data is complete.


================================================================================
PART SEVEN — SHEEP IN THE SAME SHED AS GOATS
================================================================================

Shed 15 has goats and sheep. Both groups have work due in the same week.

The system plans separate drives by species. Goats due for PPR get a goat drive;
sheep due for PPR get a sheep drive. Sheep follow their own matrix including Blue
Tongue and combinations that differ from goats. Operators may visit the same shed
twice or run two product lines on the same day, but the system never merges goat
and sheep into one vaccination batch.


================================================================================
PART EIGHT — WHEN DATA IS MISSING
================================================================================

A kid is registered without a date of birth.

The system cannot place birth-based doses on a calendar. Work is held with a
visible reason: missing date of birth. Nothing is silently skipped. When DOB is
added later, the hold is cleared and real due dates are generated.

An adult arrives without a reliable entry or warming date.

The same idea applies: hold with missing arrival information until intake data
is fixed, then the adult procurement schedule can run.


================================================================================
PART NINE — LATE DOSES AND PHC REVIEW
================================================================================

A dose window passes and the animal was sick, or the drive was missed.

Depending on the rule, the system may flag catch-up for PHC approval rather than
automatically injecting on the next day. Adherence shows why it is late. PHC can
approve the catch-up path. The system avoids dumping every missed primary from
months ago onto the animal in one shot; it uses a controlled catch-up approach.


================================================================================
PART TEN — MOVING SHEDS, LEAVING THE FARM, AND RULE CHANGES
================================================================================

A goat is moved from Shed 12 to Shed 18 while PPR is still due.

Open vaccination work moves with the goat. The next drive is planned under Shed
18, not the old shed.

If the goat is sold, dies, or leaves the herd, new doses are not created. Open
work is closed appropriately. History of completed doses remains for audit.

When leadership publishes a new vaccination rule version in Config, animals
already in the herd are rechecked against the new rules. Work tied to old versions
is handled according to version policy; completed history stays tied to the
version that created it.


================================================================================
PART ELEVEN — MANUAL CAMPAIGNS
================================================================================

PHC runs a special campaign after an outbreak or audit.

That is separate from the day-to-day clock. An approved campaign can create
extra work for a defined group without changing the base matrix for every animal
on the farm. Normal scheduling continues alongside for everyone else.


================================================================================
PART TWELVE — WHAT THE SYSTEM DOES NOT DO TODAY
================================================================================

Please read this section carefully before sign-off.

The system does not run a second medical check on every goat at the exact moment
a drive is built. Holds must be correct in health and location data before the
planning pass. If a goat is marked healthy but is actually sick on the ground,
operations discipline still matters.

The system does not use vaccine expiry to pick the drive date. Expiry is handled
when stock is reserved (first expiry first out), not when choosing which Tuesday
to visit the shed.

We do not support the early kid schedule for "mother not vaccinated" in this
release. Unknown mother is treated as vaccinated.

Combo visits align the same date for two products (for example FMD and HS) but may
still show as separate product tasks in the system, even though the team does one
visit.

The full vaccine matrix must be published in Config for each environment. Presets
exist; someone with authority must publish the rules before animals get real due
dates.


================================================================================
PART THIRTEEN — RULES WE ARE ASKING YOU TO APPROVE
================================================================================

Please confirm these operating choices:

1. Each animal is scheduled individually before any shed drive is planned.

2. Mother unknown means use the mother-vaccinated (later) kid timings.

3. Maximum two vaccine products on one combo visit, never three.

4. Goats and sheep in the same shed get separate drives.

5. Seven days warming after arrival with no vaccination.

6. Sick, ICU, and quarantine mean hold until cleared, visible in adherence.

7. Pregnancy months four and five hold; catch up within two weeks after delivery.

8. Procured adults use the short intake schedule, not the full kid week table.

9. Trusted source vaccination can skip duplicates; warming still applies.

10. Small sheds may merge at park level; lone animals still get a drive eventually.

11. Combo visits may share a date but appear as separate product tasks — acceptable
    if the team still does one physical visit.

12. Drive planning does not score by vial expiry — acceptable if FEFO at reserve
    is enough for operations.

PART FIFTEEN — SUGGESTED STAGING WALKTHROUGH AFTER APPROVAL
================================================================================

After sign-off, we recommend walking these six situations on staging with real
shed names and dates, not only reading this document:

1. Farm-born kid from first ET+TT through first FMD+HS visit.
2. Procured adult after warming through day-zero and week-four waves.
3. One animal sick between doses, then recovered and driven.
4. One singleton shed and whether park merge or small drive happens.
5. One mixed goat-and-sheep shed with two separate drives.
6. One late dose showing PHC review or catch-up.
